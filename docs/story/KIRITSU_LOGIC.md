# KIRITSU — story logic and final-choice mechanics

> The hard contract behind the plot. If a scene contradicts this file, the scene
> is wrong. No romance; the emotional engine is institutional betrayal,
> identity, and the cost of order.

## 1. The one-sentence premise

The city is a single legal document. Cursor 07 is the auditor whose **open
identity line** is the last unresolved record in District 9. To close the
district he must execute a self-deletion order that only his own keystroke
signature can validate. Refusing does not free him: it rolls the district back
and erases the people he is trying to save. His mentor, Kuro, released the
order and cannot execute it for him.

## 2. World rules (must stay consistent)

### R1 — The city is a ledger
Every citizen, building, train, debt, and memory is a line in the KIRITSU
ledger. "Physical" reality is the ledger being rendered. Editing a line changes
the city.

### R1b — The frozen 06:23
The city clock is frozen at **06:23**. The first identity line was written at
**2006-06-23 06:23**; the build is `2006.06.23`. The nine-minute audit window
runs from 06:23 to 06:32. CASE 0623 is named for the date, not the loop count.
The date is an in-world origin record, never framed as the player's birthday.

### R2 — Identity has a signature
A citizen's core identity line is sealed with a **keystroke motif**: a personal
pattern of timing and keys, learned by the Bureau. A change to a core identity is
valid only when the motif matches. Admin overrides are marked **review**, not
**authorship**.

### R3 — Self-closure clause
A core identity line can only be **closed** by the subject's own authored edit.
No auditor, director, or system process may delete another person's core line
without invalidating the district's closure. This clause was written to prevent
tyranny: no one can erase a person without their own hand.

### R4 — District closure
A district can advance only when every line is **closed** and its closure hash
matches the master ledger. An unresolved line keeps the district in quarantine.

### R5 — The open line is the signing key
KIRITSU's audit signature is generated from the **auditor's open identity
line**. A registered (closed) auditor cannot sign an identity closure: a closed
line has no open field. This is why the Bureau keeps one auditor unregistered.

### R6 — External deletion is noise
If a line is deleted by anyone other than its subject, the signature mismatch
marks it as **forged**. A forged line cannot be closed; the district resets.

### R7 — Dead Man's Loop
If the district is not closed inside the **9-minute audit window**, KIRITSU
assumes corruption and rolls District 9 back to its last checkpoint. The reset
erases all unregistered lines (people), restores the registered snapshot, and
wipes the auditor's episodic memory. The auditor retains only muscle memory.
This has happened many times.

### R8 — The auditor cannot leave the buffer
A cursor has no body. The only exits are: complete closure, erase the protocol,
expose the audit, or create a new identity line (AWAKEN). "Walking away" is not a
valid state; it is a loop reset.

### R9 — Kuro's authority is review, not authorship
Kuro can **release** a case into Cursor 07's queue and sign it as reviewer. He
cannot type 07's motif. Any attempt to execute the deletion himself creates an
invalid closure and triggers R7.

### R10 — The last unresolved line
District 9's only unresolved line is Cursor 07's own origin record. He was taken
from the unregistered as a child and deliberately never registered so that he
could serve as the open-line auditor. His existence is the district's remaining
error. That is why the final case is his own file.

## 3. Why the order exists

`CASE 0623 // SELF-CLOSURE` is auto-generated by KIRITSU when a district has one
open line and that line belongs to the auditor. It is addressed to the auditor
because:

- the open line's owner is the only valid signer;
- Kuro's release signature is required to put it in the queue;
- the action must be typed in the subject's own motif.

So the order is not "Kuro orders 07 to die." It is the system's legal form for
"this district can only close if its last open person consents." Kuro releases
it because he wants the loop to end. He hopes 07 will choose a third path; he
cannot choose for him.

The text on the order is deliberately bureaucratic:

```text
KIRITSU // DISTRICT 9 // CASE 0623
SUBJECT: CURSOR 07
STATUS: OPEN LINE (LAST)
ACTION: SELF-CLOSURE
REVIEW: KURO
EXECUTE: [SUBJECT MOTIF REQUIRED]
```

## 4. Why 07 must delete himself

1. District 9 cannot close while any line is open (R4).
2. The last open line is 07's origin record (R10).
3. Only the subject can close a core identity (R3).
4. Only 07 has the motif for that line (R2).
5. Any external deletion is forged (R6) and triggers a reset (R7).
6. Therefore the only **legal** closure is 07 deleting his own line.

This is not a chosen-one prophecy. It is a bureaucratic trap built from a
protective law. The horror is that the rule designed to protect identity is
being used to demand consent to erasure.

## 5. Why he cannot refuse

Refusal is not an exit. It is a reset.

- The 9-minute window closes (R7).
- District 9 rolls back to checkpoint.
- The unregistered people he met (including RIN, the child in the lower
  market) are erased as noise.
- 07's memory is wiped; he wakes at the start of the same loop.
- Kuro releases the same case again.

ORACLE logs the loop count. The first time the player sees it, the count is
already **4,217**. The 9-minute memory gap at the start is the residue of the
last reset. 07 has refused thousands of times; each refusal killed the district
again.

There is also no physical escape: a cursor cannot leave the buffer (R8). He can
only change the **form** of closure.

## 6. Why Kuro does this

Kuro is the Chief Auditor of District 9. He is old, tired, and complicit. He
took 07 from the unregistered quarter as a child, trained him, and wiped his
memory after every loop to keep him functional. He did not do this because he
enjoys cruelty. He did it because:

- he believes the city will collapse without closure;
- he cannot execute the order himself (R9);
- he cannot bear to watch 07 remember the loop and break;
- he wants 07 to have a choice, even if the choice is monstrous.

Kuro's betrayal is **institutional care**: he loves the city and the child in
his own controlling way, and he has decided that the child's consent is the only
clean way out. His review signature on CASE 0623 is a confession, not a command.

## 7. Why 07 orders it himself

The order is **addressed to him by KIRITSU**, but it becomes executable only
when he moves the cursor and types the closure command. It looks self-issued
because:

- he is both auditor and subject;
- the case is generated from his own open line;
- the only signature KIRITSU will accept is his own.

Kuro cannot forge it. ORACLE cannot sign it. The Registrar cannot type it. The
player is the only hand in the system.

The final interface is intentionally the same cursor the player has used all
game. The order is not a cutscene; it is a buffer.

## 8. Why not just edit the order?

The order is a **closure form**, not ordinary text. It validates:

- the subject hash;
- the review signature;
- the action hash (`SELF-CLOSURE`);
- the subject's keystroke motif.

Arbitrary edits fail the action hash and are rejected as noise. But there are
four structurally valid edits, and each is an ending:

### COMPLY — execute the closure
Type `dd` on the subject line. The district closes. The unregistered are
registered as numbers and survive; they lose the right to be unregistered. 07
is deleted. Kuro signs the completed audit and retires. The city is stable and
smaller. This is the "order" ending.

### AUDIT — copy the evidence out
Refuse the signature; copy CASE 0623, the unregistered list, and Kuro's review
signature into the public ledger/register. The district resets, but the evidence
leaves the quarantine. 07 is wiped; the next iteration may find the truth
outside. RIN may die in the reset unless her line is copied into a register.
This is the truth-at-a-cost ending.

### ERASE — delete the protocol
The city charter is a separate root document. Delete KIRITSU itself: all lines
become unregistered, all closures are void, transit and life support lose their
scheduling. The unregistered are free and exposed. Kuro dies with the Bureau.
07 may survive as an unregistered cursor outside the city. This is the freedom
ending, not a happy one.

### AWAKEN — create a new line
Before confirming `dd`, inspect the undo buffer. It contains the **original
child identity** Kuro saved from the first loop: 07's name before he was made
an auditor. Insert it as a new identity line and register it under his own
motif. The open line closes as a **new person**, not a deletion. District 9 can
close without killing him. The unregistered remain unregistered, but the
district no longer needs a sacrifice. The final chapter title changes from
**規律** to **起立**. This is the hidden/awakening ending.

The final line of the game is not `:%d` or `:wq`. It is the player's choice of
which of these four structures to write.

## 9. What the player should do (spoiler-free guidance)

At the final order:

1. **Read all three files first**: own record, unregistered list, charter. The
   order is designed to make you act before reading.
2. **Do not assume deletion is the only lawful act**. The interface allows
   copying evidence, deleting the root, or using the undo buffer. The game has
   taught all four.
3. **Understand the reset**: refusing without copying evidence kills the
   district again. Refusal alone is not heroism.
4. **Choose what "order" means**: a closed city, a exposed truth, a free ruin,
   or a new person. There is no clean save.

## 11. Name meaning in-world

- **規律 (きりつ)**: order, discipline, rule. The official reading.
- **起立 (きりつ)**: standing up. The hidden reading.
- The Bureau's backronym: **Kernel Integrity Regulation & Inspection Terminal —
  Standard Unit**.
- The system never notices the homophone. The player does.

## 12. Continuity / trap checklist

- No romance; no love interest. The bond is mentor/ward and a child (RIN).
- Kuro is not a cartoon villain; he is a tired bureaucrat who chose control.
- The Registrar is not human; it has no malice, only closure.
- 07's deletion is not a prophecy; it is a legal trap.
- Refusal alone is a loop, not an ending.
- The four endings follow from the four structural edits; no deus ex machina.
- The hidden ending requires the player to have read the undo buffer and the
  original identity line, not a high score.
- No book prose, no real-world Tokyo trademarks, no impersonation of a real
  company.
